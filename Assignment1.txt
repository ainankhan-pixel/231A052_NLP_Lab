1. Introduction

Smart Replies are an application of Natural Language Processing (NLP) that automatically generates short, relevant responses to messages. These suggestions help users reply quickly without typing a complete message.

Smart Reply systems analyse the incoming message, identify its intent and context, and suggest suitable responses. For example, when someone asks, "Are you available for a meeting tomorrow?", a smart reply system might suggest "Yes, I am available" or "Sorry, I am busy tomorrow."

Smart Replies are used in applications such as Gmail and LinkedIn. Depending on the application, they may use language models, machine learning, contextual analysis, and other NLP techniques.

Objective: To study a real-world Smart Reply application, compare the responses generated by two tools, identify their limitations, and suggest improvements for an Indian language.

2.4 Successes and Failures

Successes:

Smart Replies can reduce the time required to respond to routine messages.

They can suggest grammatically correct and polite responses.

They are useful for common situations such as confirmations, acknowledgements, and simple requests.

Short suggestions can improve communication efficiency.

Possible failures:

A short response may ignore important details from the original message.

The tool may misunderstand sarcasm, humour, or ambiguous statements.

4. Part (iii): Adapting Smart Replies for Hindi

4.1 Introduction

Hindi is widely used in India, both independently and in combination with English. To make Smart Reply systems useful for Hindi-speaking users, the system must understand Hindi grammar, vocabulary, context, politeness, and variations in everyday communication.

A system designed primarily for English may struggle with Devanagari text, Romanized Hindi, regional expressions, and messages that mix Hindi and English.

4.2 Required Linguistic Resources

1. Hindi text corpus

A large and diverse collection of Hindi messages, emails, conversations, and question-answer pairs is needed to train and evaluate the system. Data should be collected with appropriate consent and privacy protection.

2. Tokenizer

A Hindi-aware tokenizer should split text into meaningful units while handling Devanagari characters, punctuation, and mixed-language text.

3. Morphological analyser

Hindi words can change according to gender, number, tense, and grammatical agreement. Morphological analysis can help generate grammatically appropriate responses.

4. Part-of-Speech (POS) tagger

A POS tagger identifies nouns, verbs, adjectives, pronouns, and other grammatical categories. This information can support sentence analysis.

5. Named Entity Recognition (NER)

An NER system identifies names of people, places, organisations, dates, and times so that important information is preserved in the response.

6. Hindi language model

A pretrained multilingual or Hindi-capable language model can be adapted to generate short, relevant replies. It should be evaluated on real Hindi and code-switched examples.

7. Parallel and code-switched datasets

Hindi-English parallel text and code-switched conversations can help the system understand messages such as "Kal assignment submit karna hai" and "Please notes bhej do."

4.3 Socio-Linguistic Challenges

1. Code-switching

Users frequently mix Hindi and English in a single sentence, such as "Meeting kal hai, please confirm kar dena." The system must recognise both languages and preserve the intended meaning.

2. Regional dialects

Hindi varies across regions and communities. Common expressions, pronunciation-based spellings, and vocabulary may differ. Training data should represent these variations.

3. Formality and respect

Hindi uses different levels of politeness, such as "तुम" and "आप." A reply to a professor or senior colleague should generally maintain a respectful tone.

5. Gender and grammatical agreement

Hindi adjectives and verbs can vary according to gender and number. The system should avoid inappropriate grammatical forms and use neutral constructions when the user's identity is unknown.

6. Cultural context

Greetings such as "Namaste," references to festivals, and culturally specific expressions may require contextual understanding. Literal translation alone may not preserve their intended tone.

4.4 Concrete Steps for Adaptation

Collect a diverse Hindi dataset containing routine messages, requests, confirmations, acknowledgements, and academic conversations.

Obtain consent for data collection, remove personal information, and protect private messages.

Preprocess the data by removing duplicates, normalising Unicode, handling punctuation, and identifying the language and script.

Develop support for both Devanagari Hindi and Romanized Hindi.

Fine-tune or adapt a suitable pretrained multilingual or Hindi language model using examples paired with appropriate short replies.

Include formal, informal, and respectful response styles.

Test the system on regional expressions, code-switched sentences, spelling variations, and culturally specific messages.

Evaluate response relevance, grammatical correctness, context preservation, cultural appropriateness, and response latency.

Ask Hindi speakers from different backgrounds to review the generated responses.

Provide multiple suggestions and allow users to edit or reject them before sending.

5. Conclusion

Smart Replies demonstrate how NLP can improve everyday communication by generating short responses to incoming messages. They can save time and provide useful suggestions for routine conversations, but their quality depends on language understanding, context, and the user's intended tone.

Comparing two tools using the same input messages helps identify differences in relevance, grammatical quality, context preservation, and language support. The comparison should be based on actual outputs and documented with screenshots.

Adapting Smart Replies for Hindi requires suitable linguistic resources, support for multiple scripts, handling of code-switching and regional variations, and awareness of cultural conventions. Human evaluation and privacy protection are also essential for developing a reliable system.
